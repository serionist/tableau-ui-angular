Dialogs

A dialog shows information or asks the user to decide, above the page.

Import

Inject DialogService from tableau-ui-angular/dialog: one for the app, so Escape closes the top dialog. On the server it shows nothing, and a confirmation resolves false at once.

Confirmation with a message

openConfirmationMessageDialog() resolves true when the user accepts. Its colour styles the accept button, and autofocus picks the button focus goes to as it opens, accept or cancel; without it, focus goes to cancel, the first.

Confirmation with a template

openConfirmationTemplateDialog() shows a template as the content, and takes the buttons' texts.

A component of its own

openModal() shows any component, with no buttons of its own. The component reads its data with injectDialogData() and closes with injectDialogRef(); closed$ gives the result. A modal without a header is named by label in its args, as the second dialog here is.

Overlays in a modal

A modal sits in the browser's top layer, above every z-index of the page, and the page outside it is inert: an overlay outside the modal shows under it and takes no clicks or focus. So a tooltip, a snack, or a dialog the app opens with openDialog() or openTemplateDialog(), opened while a modal is open, goes into that modal: it sits above the modal's content and takes its clicks and focus. A select's list, an autocomplete's suggestions and a menu sit after their element, so in the modal already. A modal that closes first closes the dialogs in it, top first, and the snacks go back to the page. A modal opened from a modal, a confirmation included, goes to the body above it, so a modal can open the next one and close itself.

$overlayHost() is where an overlay opened now belongs: the topmost modal's overlay layer, div.dialog-overlay-layer at the end of its container, or null while no modal is open, for the body. The layer takes no space and sits above everything in the modal's content, a sticky toolbar or a map with a z-index of its own included. An app that appends an overlay of its own to the body appends it to $overlayHost() ?? document.body instead, position: fixed at the viewport coordinates getBoundingClientRect() gives: an absolute one is placed against the layer, which has no size, so page coordinates land low by the page's scroll and a percentage size is of nothing. When the modal closes, the service moves what the app put in the layer on to the next host, the topmost modal still open or the body; the app removes what should go with the modal. The overlays are position: fixed in the modal's container, so a transform, filter or contain on .dialog-container would move and clip them.

Accessibility

A modal follows the WAI-ARIA dialog (modal) pattern: it is a <dialog> the service shows with showModal(), so the browser makes the rest of the page inert for the keyboard, the pointer and screen readers until it closes.

  • Screen readers hear a modal dialog named by its header's title. A modal without a header is named by label in its args; a dialog that isn't a modal, from openDialog() or openTemplateDialog(), ignores it. A confirmation is described by its message, which a screen reader reads with the button focus goes to.
  • As a modal opens, focus goes to the first of these: an element its content focused as it rendered, its content's first autofocus element, its first element in the Tab order, the header's close button, the modal itself. Content opened from a modal marks the element to focus autofocus rather than focusing it as it renders: until the new modal shows, it is outside the open one, inert, and takes no focus. A modal the content opens as it renders, such as a confirmation from its ngOnInit, shows above it.
  • When it closes, focus goes back to the element focused before it opened, while that is still in the page. A modal that closes under one it opened, as a wizard's step does, leaves focus in the one still open, which gives it back there in its turn. A modal opened from a menu's item, as the menu closes, gives focus back to the menu's button; a menu opened with trapFocus false, or a button gone by the time the modal closes, leaves focus on the body.
  • A dialog that isn't a modal and keeps focus, such as a menu or one the app opens with trapFocus, keeps Tab in itself, and in the dialogs opened above it, such as one a menu item opens: Tab and Shift+Tab move as the browser moves them, a date input's own fields included, and wrap at either end. It gives focus back when it closes, and the page's own Tab order stays as it is.
  • Its backdrop is the <dialog>'s ::backdrop, in --twc-modal-color-backdrop; style it as dialog.dialog-container::backdrop.
Key or press What it does
Escape Closes the top dialog in the modal, such as a select's list, then the modal, unless closeOnEscape is false. A showing tooltip takes it first. A handler of the app's, on an element in the modal or on the document, keeps the modal open with preventDefault(). Escape in a date or time input closes nothing, since it can come from closing the input's own picker
Tab, Shift+Tab Move through the modal's elements; the page outside takes no focus
A press on the backdrop Closes the modal, unless closeOnBackdropClick is false. The press starts and ends on the backdrop, so a text selection dragged out of the modal keeps it
